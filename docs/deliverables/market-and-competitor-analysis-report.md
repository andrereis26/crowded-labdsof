# Market and Competitor Analysis Report

## 1. Relevant Alternatives

### 1.1. Bandsintown

Bandsintown helps users discover concerts based on their location and musical preferences. It supports artist following, music-service connections, personalised recommendations and event alerts, directly overlapping with Crowded’s discovery features.

- **Target User:** music fans, artist and promoters

### 1.2. Eventbrite

Eventbrite combines event discovery, registration and ticketing with organiser tools, including attendee communication and check-in. Its application also allows users to access tickets and connect with friends.

- **Change Handling:**  when the event is cancelled or postponed, its policy requires the organiser to tell attendees through Eventbrite's email too.
- **Target User:** small and mid-sizes organisers and their attendees.

### 1.3. Ticketline and BOL

Ticketline and Bol provides online and physical ticket sales, ticketing management and access-control services in Portugal. It also communicates cancellations and postponements to online buyers.

- **Changes Handling:** mostly by email and through news on their websites
- **Target User:** buyers of paid events

### 1.4. DICE

A mobile-first ticketing platform focused on music. Tickets are locked inside the app, and the QR code only appears shortly before the event. It offers a waiting list and fan-to-fan resale.

### 1.5. Official Festival Apps

Apps built for a single event. They offer the line-up, maps, accessibility and mobility information, and push notifications about schedule changes.

- **Limit:** each app covers only one event. They are installed and abandoned every year, and small organisers cannot afford schedule changes.

### 1.6. Municipal Agendas

The official agenda of public and cultural events, publish as open data with an API.

- **Limit:** a list only, with no notifications or on-site information.

### 1.7. Social Media

The organiser's default channel for urgent updates. Whatsapp Communities now support events, RSVPs, remainderrs and replies to announcements.

- **Limit:** information is scattered, there is no source or timestamp, and organisers cannot know who received the message

## 2. Strengths and Weaknesses

| Alternatives | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| -------- | -------- | -------- |
| **Bandsintown** | Personalised concert discovery; music-service connections; configurable mobile search radius; artist following and alerts | Primarily focused on live music; ticket purchases can involve external services; structured event-specific crowd reporting was not established |
| **Eventbrite** | Discovery, registration and ticket access within one platform; organiser tools; attendee communication; friend connections | Documented ticket management centres on Eventbrite orders; external ticket import and structured live crowd reports were not established |
| **Ticketline and BOL** | Relevant Portuguese ticketing infrastructure; online and physical distribution; access control; cancellation and postponement communication | Operational access information does not necessarily provide public crowd conditions; attendee matching and cross-provider ticket storage were not established |
| **DICE** | Tickets in the app; waiting list; organiser pays the fees | Covers only its own tickets; focused on music; limited presence in Portugal |
| **Official Festival App** | Rich on-site information; push notifications | Per place, not per event; no data for one-off events; no queues or alerts |
| **Municipal Agendas** | Official public events, including free ones | Listing only, with no notifications or on-site information |
| **Social Media** | Existing audiences and relationships; group communication; event coordination; polls and rapid information sharing | Information can become fragmented across groups and pages; informal reports are difficult to aggregate and keep current; visibility and privacy vary by context |

## 3. Opportunity for differentiation 

### 3.1. One place for every event

Crowded can bring together tickets from any provider, free public events and events followed via QR code. No competitor covers all three. The wallets come closest, but they depend on the issuer and ignore free events.

### 3.2. Provenance as the core feature

Every item would carry its source and its last-update time. Stale information would be flagged or hidden. None of the alternatives does this, and it directly answers both the noise on social media and the low trust in Portuguese ticketing.

### 3.3. "Festival-app" features for every organiser

Crowd levels, queues, alerts and incidents would be available to any organiser through a simple backoffice or an API. Today only large festivals reach this level. Small associations and municipalities do not.

### 3.4. A direct channel to the audience during the event

Organisers could alert everyone connected to the event: tickets holders, followers and people who scanned the QR code. Unlike Whatsapp or Instagram, they would also get delivery confirmation.

### 3.5. Attendance-intention polls for capacity planning

Polls produce demand data. Crowded would offer this to any organiser, not only to those using one ticketing platform. Result must be presented as indications, not guaranteed attendence.
