# Market and Competitor Analysis Report

## 1. Relevant Alternatives

### 1.1. Bandsintown

Bandsintown helps users discover concerts based on their location and musical preferences. It supports artist following, music-service connections, personalized recommendations and event alerts. Through its Manager app, artists can notify fans who RSVP'd about cancellations or venue changes [1].

- **Change Handling:** depends on the artist or manager sending the message.
- **Target Users:** music fans, artists and promoters.
- **Business Model:** advertising, commissions on ticket sales and paid tools for venues and promoters [2].
- **User Experience:** good for following artists and receiving concert alerts, but alerts can fail and it offers little once the event begins.
- **Privacy and Trust:** recommendations rely on listening data from connected music services.

### 1.2. Songkick

Songkick is a concert discovery platform that lets users track their favorite artists and receive alerts when they announce concerts nearby. It connects with music services to find the artists a user listens to, and offers tools for artists to manage and promote their tour dates [3][4].

- **Target Users:** music fans and artists.
- **Business Model:** referral fees from ticketing companies when users buy tickets through its listings [3].
- **User Experience:** simple artist tracking and local concert alerts, but users have reported difficulty sharing an event with friends.
- **Privacy and Trust:** artist tracking relies on data from connected music services.

### 1.3. Eventbrite

Eventbrite combines event discovery, registration and ticketing with organizer tools, including attendee communication and check-in. Its application also allows users to access tickets and connect with friends.

- **Change Handling:** when the event is cancelled or postponed, its policy requires the organizer to tell attendees through Eventbrite's email tool [5][6].
- **Target Users:** small and mid-sized organizers and their attendees.
- **Business Model:** fees charged on tickets sold [5].
- **User Experience:** purchase and ticket access in one app, but changes arrive by email and searching can be difficult.
- **Privacy and Trust:** its policy requires refunds, including fees, when an event is cancelled [5], but communication depends on the organizer.

### 1.4. Ticketline and BOL

Ticketline and BOL provide online and physical ticket sales, ticketing management and access-control services in Portugal. They also communicate cancellations and postponements to online buyers.

- **Change Handling:** mostly by email and through news on their websites.
- **Target Users:** buyers of paid events and the promoters who sell through them.
- **Business Model:** ticket sales on behalf of promoters and venues.
- **User Experience:** focused on the purchase, with little support after the ticket is bought.
- **Privacy and Trust:** official sellers, but there are public complaints about communication and refunds after cancellations [7][8].

### 1.5. DICE

A mobile-first ticketing platform focused on music. Tickets are locked inside the app, and the QR code only appears shortly before the event [9]. It offers a waiting list and fan-to-fan resale.

- **Target Users:** fans of electronic, hip-hop and contemporary music, and the promoters and venues of those scenes.
- **Business Model:** organizers pay a percentage of ticket revenue; fans pay no booking fees [9].
- **User Experience:** mobile-first and simple, but tickets can only be used inside the app.
- **Privacy and Trust:** tickets locked to the app limit screenshots and resale outside the platform [9].

### 1.6. Official Festival Apps

Apps built for a single event (e.g., NOS Alive, Rock in Rio Lisboa). They offer the line-up, maps, accessibility and mobility information, and push notifications about schedule changes [10][11].

- **Limit:** each app covers only one event. They are installed and abandoned every year, and small organizers cannot afford their own app.
- **Target Users:** attendees of a specific large festival.
- **Business Model:** funded by the festival organizers and their sponsors.
- **User Experience:** rich information during the festival, but a new app has to be installed for each event.
- **Privacy and Trust:** official information comes directly from the organizer.

### 1.7. Municipal Agendas

The official agenda of public and cultural events, published as open data with an API [12].

- **Limit:** a list only, with no notifications or on-site information.
- **Target Users:** residents and visitors looking for public, often free, events.
- **Business Model:** public funding from the municipality.
- **User Experience:** a searchable listing, with no personalization or alerts.
- **Privacy and Trust:** an official institutional source.

### 1.8. Social Media

The organizer's default channel for urgent updates. WhatsApp Communities now support events, RSVPs, reminders and replies to announcements [13].

- **Limit:** information is scattered, there is no source or timestamp, and organizers cannot know who received the message.
- **Target Users:** the general public, organizers who already have an audience on these platforms.
- **Business Model:** advertising.
- **User Experience:** familiar and fast, but updates get lost among unrelated content.
- **Privacy and Trust:** it is hard to distinguish official information from rumor, and visibility depends on each group or page.

### 1.9. Festing

A Lisbon-based platform that combines a curated event feed (filtered by vibe, genre or neighborhood) with a social layer for each event. Users see who is going, share photos, plan the night together and send "waves" to other attendees. Organizers have a free portal to publish events and build a community, and users receive notifications from the organizers they follow. Feeds are monitored with AI [14].

- **Target Users:** young adults looking for curated events and new people to go with, small local organizers.
- **Limit:** focused on discovery and community before the event, it does not handle external tickets, official change history, crowd levels, queues or incidents.
- **Business Model:** free for users and organizers, with no public monetization model [14].
- **User Experience:** a curated feed with social interaction around each event.
- **Privacy and Trust:** AI-moderated feeds; attendance and profiles are visible to other attendees [14].

## 2. Strengths and Weaknesses

| Alternatives | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| -------- | -------- | -------- |
| **Bandsintown** | Personalized concert discovery; music-service connections; configurable mobile search radius; artist following and alerts | Primarily focused on live music; ticket purchases can involve external services; no crowd or queue information |
| **Songkick** | Artist tracking; local concert alerts; music-service connections; global concert listings | Primarily focused on live music; tickets bought through external sellers; no crowd or queue information |
| **Eventbrite** | Discovery, registration and ticket access within one platform; organizer tools; attendee communication; friend connections | Ticket management centers on Eventbrite orders; changes communicated mainly by email; no external ticket import or live crowd information |
| **Ticketline and BOL** | Relevant Portuguese ticketing infrastructure; online and physical distribution; access control; cancellation and postponement communication | Changes communicated mainly by email; public complaints about communication after cancellations [7][8]; no channel to attendees during the event |
| **DICE** | Tickets in the app; waiting list; organizer pays the fees | Covers only its own tickets; focused on music; limited presence in Portugal |
| **Official Festival App** | Rich on-site information; push notifications about schedule changes | One app per event; abandoned after the event; costly to build, so not viable for small organizers |
| **Municipal Agendas** | Official public events, including free ones; open data | Listing only, with no notifications or on-site information |
| **Social Media** | Existing audiences and relationships; group communication; event coordination; polls and rapid information sharing | Information can become fragmented across groups and pages; informal reports are difficult to aggregate and keep current; visibility and privacy vary by context |
| **Festing** | Local (Lisbon); curated discovery; strong social layer per event; free organizer portal; notifications from followed organizers; AI moderation | Focused on community, not operations; no external tickets; no change history, crowd levels, queues or incidents; no source or timestamp on information |

## 3. Opportunity for Differentiation

### 3.1. One place for every event

Crowded can bring together tickets from any provider and free public events. Ticketing platforms such as Eventbrite, Ticketline, BOL and DICE only cover their own tickets. Festing covers free events but not tickets bought elsewhere. No competitor covers both.

### 3.2. Provenance as the core feature

Every item would carry its source (official source, organization, participant, AI) and its last-update time. Stale information would be flagged or hidden. Festing verifies organizers, but no alternative labels each piece of information with its source and update time. This answers both the noise on social media and the public complaints about how Portuguese ticketing communicates changes.


### 3.3. "Festival-app" features for every organizer

Crowd levels, queues, alerts and incidents would be available to any organizer through a simple backoffice or an API. Today only large festivals with their own app reach this level. Festing's free portal helps organizers build a community, but not manage on-site conditions.

### 3.4. A direct channel to the audience during the event

Organizers could alert everyone connected to the event: ticket holders and followers. Unlike WhatsApp, Instagram or Festing's notifications to followers, the alerts would be tied to the specific event and its current state, and organizers would see how many people received them.

### 3.5. Attendance-intention polls for capacity planning

Bandsintown RSVPs and Festing's "who's going" already show interest in confirmed events. Crowded would let organizers poll interest in a proposed event (artist, date or location) before finalizing capacity, staff and entrances. Results must be presented as indications, not guaranteed attendance.

## 4. Value Proposition

For people attending events in Portugal and the organizations that run them, Crowded keeps every event they are connected to up to date until it ends. It brings together official changes, crowd levels, queues and organizer alerts, each showing where it came from and when it was last updated.

Unlike ticketing platforms, which stop at the ticket, discovery and social apps such as Bandsintown, Songkick and Festing, which focus on the time before the event, and social media, where information is scattered and unverified, Crowded works with tickets from any provider and with free events, and gives every organizer a direct channel to their audience when something changes or goes wrong.
